description: People picked the more attractive of two faces, then explained their choice. Sometimes they were secretly handed the other face. How many noticed? Guess.

meta:
- '2005'
- Lund University, Sweden
- 120 participants
story:
- Participants saw pairs of photos of women’s faces and pointed at the one they found more attractive. The researcher then slid the chosen photo across the table and asked them to explain their choice.
- 'In 3 of the 15 pairs, the researcher used a magician’s trick: the photo handed over was the face the participant had not chosen. Some people had 2 seconds to decide, some 5, and some as long as they liked.'
you:

type: percent
answer: 13
q: What percentage of the swapped photos did people notice right away?
you_real: In the real study, only 13% of the swapped photos were noticed right away.
original: Out of 354 swapped trials, 46 (13%) were spotted on the spot. Counting every kind of detection, including people who said afterwards that something had seemed odd, the total was no more than 26%.
today: 'This “choice blindness” has since been shown with the taste of jam and the smell of tea at a supermarket stall, and with opinions: in a 2012 survey on moral questions, 69% of participants failed to notice at least one of two answers that had been reversed.'
surprise: People explained choices they had never made with the same confidence as the choices they had really made.
