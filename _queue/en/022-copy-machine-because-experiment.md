story:
- At a library photocopier at the City University of New York, a researcher waited until someone started copying, then walked up and asked to go first. The request came in three versions.
- 'One gave no reason: “Excuse me, I have 5 pages. May I use the Xerox machine?” One gave a real reason: “…because I’m in a rush.” The third gave a reason that added nothing: “…because I have to make copies.” Everyone at a copier has to make copies.'

answer: 93
q: With the empty reason (“because I have to make copies”) and 5 pages, what percentage let the person go first?
you_real: 93% of the real people at the copier let the researcher go first after hearing the empty reason, almost as many as with a real reason (94%).
original: 'For 5 pages, 60% said yes when no reason was given, 94% with the real reason and 93% with the empty one. When the request was for 20 pages, the empty reason stopped working: 24% said yes, the same as with no reason at all, while the real reason got 42%.'
today: 'The study is famous but small: about 15 people per group for the 5-page request. A 1985 follow-up found that people are less mindless than this suggests: the kind of reason mattered, and some reasons worked much better than others.'
surprise: For a small favour, the word “because” did the work. Once the favour got bigger, people started listening to what came after it.
