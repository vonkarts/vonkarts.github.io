story:
- 'Researchers asked a relative of each participant for three true events from the participant’s childhood. Then they added a fourth, invented one: at about age five, the participant had got lost in a shopping mall, cried, and was found by an older person who brought them back to the family.'
- Participants received a booklet with all four stories and wrote down what they remembered about each one. Over the following weeks they were interviewed twice and asked to recall as much as they could.

q: What percentage of participants came to remember the invented event, fully or in part?
you_real: 6 of the 24 real participants (25%) came to remember being lost in the mall, fully or in part.
original: 6 of 24 people (25%) came to remember the invented event fully or partly. For years the study was cited as evidence that false memories of whole childhood events can be planted, including in court cases.
today: 'A preregistered repeat in 2023 with 123 people classed 35% as having a false memory: 8% full and 27% partial. A 2024 reanalysis of the same data that used stricter criteria brought the rate down to 4%, partly because many accounts included similar events that may really have happened.'
surprise: 'How many false memories you find depends a lot on how strictly you count them: the same data gave 35% or 4%.'
