q: Of the people who had been asked to put up the small sign two weeks earlier, what percentage agreed to the big one?
you_real: With no earlier visit, only 17% of the real homeowners agreed to the big sign. After the small window sign, 76% did.
original: 'In the group with no earlier visit, 17% said yes. Among people who had been asked to display the small safe-driving sign, 76% said yes. Even an unrelated first request, such as a “Keep California Beautiful” sign or petition, raised agreement: across those groups it was 47%.'
today: The “foot-in-the-door” effect exists, but it is smaller and less reliable than this study suggests. A 1983 review of 120 experimental groups found a small average effect, and nearly half of the studies showed no effect or one in the opposite direction. A 1999 review also found a small average effect that depends on details such as how much time passes between the two requests.
surprise: The small request and the big one came from two different people, two weeks apart.
