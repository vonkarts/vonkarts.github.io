- In the flat Sahara desert lives an ant that, after finding food, heads home in an almost straight line. It was known to find its direction from the sun. But how did it know the distance?
- Researchers trained ants to walk to a feeder 10 meters from their nest. Then, at the feeder, they lengthened the legs of some ants by gluing on thin bristles, and shortened the legs of others. And they watched the ants walk home.

original: Normal ants searched for the nest at 10 meters. Ants with lengthened legs walked past it and searched at about 15 meters; ants with shortened legs began searching at about 6 meters. The ants were measuring distance by counting their steps; when step length changed, the calculation went wrong.
today: This study is considered some of the strongest evidence that ants use a kind of pedometer. As we understand it today, ants find their direction from the sun and the light in the sky, and measure distance with their steps.
surprise: When the same ants set out from the nest on their new legs and came back, they judged the distance correctly again. What they counted was not leg length but the steps they took on that trip.
