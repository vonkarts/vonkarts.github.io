q: Among the people who had heard “smashed,” what percentage said they had seen broken glass?
you_real: Of the real participants who had heard “smashed,” 32% said they had seen broken glass; among those who had heard “hit,” it was 14%.
original: In the first experiment (45 students), “smashed” produced an average speed estimate of 40.8 mph (about 66 km/h), and “contacted” 31.8 mph (about 51 km/h). In the second (150 students), 32% of the “smashed” group reported broken glass, compared with 14% of the “hit” group and 12% of a group that was not asked about speed.
today: The study became a classic of memory research and influenced how police and courts think about eyewitness testimony and leading questions. The samples were small and made up of students, but later research has repeatedly shown that the wording of questions can change what people report remembering.
surprise: A single word, in a single question, was enough to change what some people “remembered” seeing a week later.
