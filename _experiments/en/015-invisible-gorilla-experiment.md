story:
- Students watched a short video of two teams, one in white shirts and one in black, passing basketballs. Their job was to count the passes made by one team, silently and carefully.
- Halfway through, an unexpected person walked through the middle of the game. In some versions it was someone in a full gorilla suit who stopped, faced the camera and thumped their chest; in others, a woman with an umbrella. After the video, everyone was asked whether they had noticed anything unusual.

q: What percentage of viewers did not notice the unexpected person at all?
you_real: Across all versions, 46% of the real viewers did not notice anything, not even a gorilla in the middle of the screen.
original: Across all versions, 46% of viewers did not notice the unexpected person. The harder the counting task, the more people missed it; in some versions the share of people who missed the gorilla was even higher.
today: This “inattentional blindness” has been repeated in many studies with different tasks. How many people miss the unexpected event depends a lot on how hard the task is and on how much the event looks like what they are paying attention to.
surprise: 'Experts are not immune: in 2013, a gorilla 48 times the size of a typical nodule was placed in lung CT scans. 20 of 24 radiologists (83%) did not report it, and eye tracking showed that most of them had looked right at it.'
