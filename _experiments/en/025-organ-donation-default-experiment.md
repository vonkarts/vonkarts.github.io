story:
- 161 Americans took part in an online survey about organ donation. They were asked to imagine they had just moved to a new state and had to decide whether to be an organ donor.
- The only difference between the groups was the default. In one version you were not a donor unless you chose to become one. In another you were a donor unless you chose not to be. In a third there was no default and everyone had to pick.

q: When being a donor was the default, what percentage stayed donors?
you_real: 82% of the real participants stayed donors when that was the default. When they had to opt in, only 42% became donors.
original: 'Opt-in: 42% donors. Opt-out: 82%. No default, with a forced choice: 79%. The authors saw the same pattern in Europe: in Germany, where people must opt in, 12% were registered donors, while in neighbouring Austria, where everyone is a donor unless they opt out, the figure was 99.98%.'
today: Defaults clearly change which box people end up in, but more people on a register does not automatically mean more transplants. A 2019 study of 35 OECD countries found no significant difference in deceased donors between opt-out countries (20.3 per million people) and opt-in countries (15.4 per million), and opt-out countries had fewer living donors.
surprise: Making people choose with no default (79%) gave almost the same result as making them donors by default (82%).
