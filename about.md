<p class="lead">Gut Check is a daily game built on real science. Each day it tells the story of one published experiment. You say what you would do, guess what really happened, and then see the result, the sources and what we know today.</p>

## Why
Our intuition about how people (and the world) behave is often wrong in surprising ways. Gut Check shows that gap between intuition and evidence, and also how scientific findings change over time, when studies are repeated or criticized. The site is non-commercial and has no ads.

## How experiments are chosen
- Every experiment is a real study published in a scientific journal. Topics can come from any field: psychology, medicine, economics, animals, physics, sport.
- We pick results where intuition can easily go wrong.
- Famous findings that later failed to replicate or were criticized are included on purpose, and the “What we know today” section says so clearly.

## How each page is checked
- Every number is checked against the original paper or a reliable secondary source before publishing. The sources are linked (with DOI) under each experiment so you can check them yourself.
- The story is a simplified summary; the exact conditions and details are in the original study.
- Pages about health are for information only and are not medical advice.

## Use of AI
AI tools (Claude, by Anthropic) help with research, drafting and translation into five languages. Every figure is checked against the cited sources, and the five language versions are compared sentence by sentence so they say the same thing. Experiments are prepared and checked days before they go live; the daily publishing itself is automatic.

## Who makes it
Gut Check is made by vonkarts, an independent creator writing under a pen name. It is not affiliated with any university, company or research group.

## Found a mistake?
Mistakes happen. If you spot one, please [report it on GitHub](https://github.com/vonkarts/vonkarts.github.io/issues/new) (a free GitHub account is needed). Confirmed mistakes are corrected on the page in all five languages.

## Your data
There are no accounts, no ads and no cookies. Your answers, scores and streak are stored only in your own browser and never leave it. Two things are sent to GoatCounter, a privacy-focused visitor counter: that a page was visited, and your guess for each experiment (for example “bystander, 35”), so the site can show the visitors’ average. GoatCounter does not store IP addresses, the full browser identifier or any tracking ID. Your IP address is used only briefly, to estimate your country and to avoid counting the same visit twice; this link is kept in memory for at most 8 hours and then discarded. What remains are totals: pages, countries, browser type, screen size, language and the site you came from. GoatCounter’s servers are in Finland and Germany ([privacy policy](https://www.goatcounter.com/help/privacy)). The purpose is only to know how many people the site reaches. If you don’t want to be counted, add **#sayma** to the end of the address once (vonkarts.com/#sayma); counting is then switched off in that browser. Questions or requests: [GitHub Issues](https://github.com/vonkarts/vonkarts.github.io/issues).
